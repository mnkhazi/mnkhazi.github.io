Customer Experience Framework

The 3H Framework: Hear, Honor, Heal

By Mohammed Najmuddin Khazi · 27 November 2026

The 3H Framework, Hear, Honor, Heal, is a customer experience framework created by Mohammed Najmuddin Khazi. It holds that great service is measured by whether the customer's problem was understood, respected and resolved, not by the score they gave afterwards.

The problem: service that scores well but does not solve

Most contact centres run on survey scores, handle times and ticket closure rates. These are easy to measure, so they become the goal. The result is a familiar pattern: the ticket is closed, the survey is polite, and the customer still has the same problem, or has given up.

A high CSAT can hide a customer who was worn down by repeated transfers and rated the last agent kindly. A low handle time can hide a customer who was rushed off the line. The 3H Framework starts from a different question: did this person feel heard, were they treated with respect, and is the issue actually fixed?

Hear: understand the real issue before acting

Hearing means listening for the actual problem, not just the category the customer picked or the first complaint they voiced. The stated issue and the real issue are often different.

In practice:

  • Let the customer finish before diagnosing.
  • Restate the problem back in your own words and confirm it is right.
  • Ask one question that reaches the cause, not five that follow the script.
  • Use what the customer has already told you across channels so they never have to repeat themselves.

Honor: respect the customer's experience and intent

Honoring means acknowledging that the customer's frustration, time and trust are real, and treating their request as legitimate until proven otherwise. It is the step that turns a transaction into a relationship.

In practice:

  • Acknowledge the impact on the customer before moving to the fix.
  • Give honest timelines, including when the answer is "not today".
  • Own the company's part in the problem without deflecting.
  • Give front-line teams the authority to make a fair decision on the spot.

Heal: fix the root cause, not just the ticket

Healing means the problem is resolved at its source, and where trust was damaged, it is repaired. A closed ticket is a record. A healed customer is an outcome.

In practice:

  • Resolve the issue end to end, and follow up to confirm it stayed fixed.
  • Trace repeat contacts back to process, product or policy causes and fix those.
  • Make it right when the company got it wrong, in a way proportionate to the harm.
  • Measure resolution and repeat-contact rates alongside satisfaction scores.

Why it matters to the business

The 3H Framework is a commercial argument as much as a human one. Each H connects to a number a CFO already tracks.

StepWhat it changesBusiness metric it moves
HearFewer misdiagnosed issues and repeated explanationsHandle time, transfers, first-contact resolution
HonorCustomers who feel respected stay and speak well of youRetention, complaints, referrals
HealThe same problem stops coming backRepeat contacts, cost to serve, churn

Together they shift customer experience from a cost line to a retention and growth lever.

Where it comes from

Mohammed Najmuddin Khazi is a senior operations and customer experience executive with about 27 years across banking, financial services and fintech in India and the GCC. His roles include VP-level leadership at Paytm Payments Bank and Udaan, a Director role at Flipkart, and many more.

The 3H Framework grew out of running customer operations at scale, where the gap between a good score and a solved problem is visible every day.
